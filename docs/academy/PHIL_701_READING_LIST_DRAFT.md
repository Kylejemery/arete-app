# PHIL 701: Doctoral Reading List (draft for review)

2026-10-27. A draft, for Kyle's review before any course data changes. It answers gap 1 of [DOCTORAL_AUDIT.md](DOCTORAL_AUDIT.md): real weekly reading, with page ranges, drawn from the canonical scholarship on Stoic ethics.

## How to read this

- **Keeps the course as it is.** Same fourteen sessions, same topics, same objectives. Existing primary readings stay; this adds what a doctoral seminar would read alongside them.
- **Core** is required for the session. **Further** is for the paper or a second pass.
- **Long and Sedley** (LS) is the spine for primary texts: *The Hellenistic Philosophers*, vol. 1 (Cambridge 1987), translations with commentary, cited by section. Every doctoral Stoicism syllabus surveyed uses it. It is not in the corpus; the student reads it from their own copy, as with all the scholarship below.
- **Page ranges** were read from the publishers' tables of contents (Cambridge Core, Oxford Academic, JSTOR, Project MUSE, CUP contents files) on 2026-10-27. Ranges marked † are derived (an item's end page taken as the next item's start minus one) because the source printed start pages only; every derived range checked against a printed one matched. Hadot and Graver have verified chapter numbers but no verified pages; those are cited by chapter only, to fill in from the copy in hand.
- **Load.** Core secondary reading comes to roughly 1,050 verified pages plus about seven Hadot and Graver chapters: about 80 to 100 pages a week, at the low end of the doctoral range, with LS and the ancient texts on top.

## Sessions

### 1. What is philosophy for? Hadot as entry

**Debate:** Hadot says ancient philosophy was a way of life built from spiritual exercises; Cooper agrees it was a way of life but argues that what shaped it was reasoned argument, not exercises.

- Primary: LS 26, The philosophical curriculum (pp. 158-161 †). Existing: Epictetus, Disc. I.4.
- Core: Hadot PWL ch. 3, "Spiritual Exercises". Cooper PW ch. 1, "Introduction: On Philosophy as a Way of Life" (pp. 1-23).
- Further: Hadot PWL ch. 1 and ch. 11; Cooper PW ch. 4, "Stoicism as a Way of Life" (pp. 144-225); Sellars, *The Art of Living* ch. 3 (the Stoic conception; pages unverified).

### 2. The good, the bad, and the indifferent

**Debate:** can "preferred indifferents" carry value without being goods? Ariston said the distinction collapses; Chrysippus kept it.

- Primary: LS 58, Value and indifference (pp. 354-358 †); LS 60, Good and bad (pp. 368-376 †). Existing: Ench. 1-2; DL VII.101-104 (extend to 101-107).
- Core: Brennan ch. 8, "Goods and Indifferents" (pp. 119-133). Companion ch. 9, Schofield, "Stoic Ethics" (pp. 233-256). CHHP ch. 21, Inwood and Donini, §§ v-vi, "The good" and "Values, actions and choice" (pp. 687-698).
- Further: Cicero, *De Finibus* III.50-61 (in the Library).

### 3. Impressions, assent, and the ruling faculty

**Debate:** is assent a separate act of a unified rational soul (Chrysippus), and what makes an impression "cognitive"?

- Primary: LS 39, Impressions (pp. 236-240 †); LS 53, Soul (pp. 313-322 †). Existing: Ench. 1, 5, 16, 20; Disc. I.1.
- Core: Brennan ch. 5, "Impressions and Assent" (pp. 51-61). CHHP ch. 17, Long, "Stoic psychology" (pp. 560-584 †). Frede EAP ch. 9, "Stoics and Skeptics on Clear and Distinct Impressions" (pp. 151-176).
- Further: Inwood EHA, "The Psychology of Action" (starts p. 42; the next part starts p. 105). Annas HPM chs. 2-4 (start pp. 37, 71, 89).

### 4. The discipline of desire

**Debate:** what exactly is "reservation" (hupexairesis)? A clause added to every impulse, or a standing disposition, and does it make Stoic desire safe or empty?

- Primary: existing Ench. 2, 4, 8, 14 and Disc. III.2.1-5. Add Seneca, *De Tranquillitate* 13 and *De Beneficiis* IV.34 (the reserve clause, both in the Library).
- Core: Brennan, "Reservation in Stoic Ethics", *Archiv für Geschichte der Philosophie* 82 (2000), pp. 149-177. Inwood, "Rules and Reasoning in Stoic Ethics", in Ierodiakonou (ed.), *Topics in Stoic Philosophy* (OUP 2000), pp. 95-127. CHHP ch. 21 § xi, "Determinism and ethics: impulse with reservation" (pp. 736-738). Hadot IC ch. 7, "The Discipline of Desire".
- Note: Brennan's page range is from a PhilPapers listing; the publisher page could not be read.

### 5. The discipline of action: proper functions and oikeiōsis

**Debate:** does oikeiōsis ground the content of ethics, or only explain how we come to it (Striker against Engberg-Pedersen)?

- Primary: LS 57, Impulse and appropriateness (pp. 346-353 †); LS 59, Proper functions (pp. 359-367 †). Existing: Disc. II.10; Ench. 17, 24, 30.
- Core: Striker ch. 13, "The role of oikeiōsis in Stoic ethics" (pp. 281-297). Norms ch. 6, Engberg-Pedersen, "Discovering the good: oikeiōsis and kathēkonta in Stoic ethics" (pp. 145-184 †). Brennan chs. 10-11, "Oikeiôsis and Others" and "What Makes an Action Befitting?" (pp. 154-181).
- Further: Annas MH ch. 12, "Self-Concern and the Sources and Limits of Other-Concern" (pp. 249-290); Long SS ch. 11, "Hierocles on oikeiōsis and self-perception" (pp. 250-263 †); Brennan chs. 12-13 (pp. 182-232).

### 6. The discipline of assent: philosophy as attention

**Debate:** the Academic challenge. Can a cognitive impression be told from a false one exactly like it?

- Primary: LS 40, The criteria of truth (pp. 241-252 †); LS 41, Knowledge and opinion (pp. 253-258 †). Existing: Ench. 20, 34, 45; Disc. II.18.
- Core: CHHP ch. 9, Frede, "Stoic epistemology" (pp. 295-322 †). Brennan ch. 6, "Belief and Knowledge" (pp. 62-81). Hadot IC ch. 6, "The Inner Citadel, or the Discipline of Assent".
- Further: CHHP ch. 10, Schofield, "Academic epistemology" (pp. 323-354 †); Striker ch. 2, "Kritērion tēs alētheias" (pp. 22-76).

### 7. Passions, emotions, and the eupatheiai

**Debate:** are first movements (propatheiai) part of the Stoic theory from Chrysippus, or Seneca's later defence against Posidonius (Sorabji), and how do they relate to the eupatheiai (Graver)?

- Primary: LS 65, The passions (pp. 410-422 †). Existing: DL VII.110-116; Disc. I.28.
- Core: Brennan ch. 7, "Impulses and Emotions" (pp. 82-114). Sorabji chs. 2-4, "The Emotions As Value Judgements in Chrysippus" and Seneca's two defences (pp. 29-75). Graver ch. 4, "Feelings without Assent".
- Further: Graver ch. 2, "The Pathetic Syllogism"; Norms ch. 4, M. Frede, "The Stoic doctrine of the affections of the soul" (pp. 93-112 †); Cooper RE ch. 21, "Posidonius on Emotions" (pp. 449-484); Companion ch. 10, Brennan, "Stoic Moral Psychology" (pp. 257-294).

### 8. Fate, providence, and acceptance

**Debate:** is Chrysippus' compatibilism coherent? The cylinder, the lazy argument, and what "up to us" can mean in a fully determined world (Bobzien).

- Primary: LS 54, Theology (pp. 323-332 †); LS 55, Causation and fate (pp. 333-343 †); LS 62, Moral responsibility (pp. 386-393 †). Existing: Meditations IV.3, V.8, VI.2, IX.28; Ench. 8, 17, 53; Cleanthes, Hymn to Zeus. Add Cicero, *De Fato* 39-43 (the cylinder; in the Library).
- Core: Bobzien ch. 6, "Determinism and Moral Responsibility: Chrysippus' Compatibilism" (pp. 234-329). Brennan chs. 14-16, "God and Fate", "Necessity and Responsibility", "The Lazy Argument" (pp. 235-287).
- Further: Companion ch. 7, D. Frede, "Stoic Determinism" (pp. 179-205); CHHP ch. 15, Hankinson, "Determinism and indeterminism" (pp. 513-541 †); Frede EAP ch. 8, "The Original Notion of Cause" (pp. 125-150); Bobzien ch. 7, on Epictetus and "that which depends on us" (pp. 330-357).
- Note: "amor fati" is Nietzsche's phrase, not a Stoic one; the session should say so.

### 9. The sage and the prokoptōn

**Debate:** if every non-sage is equally vicious, what is progress? Plutarch pressed the point; the Stoics answered with degrees of proximity, not of virtue.

- Primary: LS 61, Virtue and vice (pp. 377-385 †); LS 66, Ethics in action (pp. 423-428 †). Existing: Disc. I.4; Seneca, Ep. 1. Add Seneca, Ep. 75 (grades of progress; in the Library).
- Core: CHHP ch. 21 §§ ix-x, "Virtue and wisdom" and "Moral progress" (pp. 714-735). Long SS ch. 9, "The harmonics of Stoic virtue" (pp. 202-223 †).
- Further: Long SS ch. 4, "Dialectic and the Stoic sage" (pp. 85-106 †).

### 10. Living according to nature

**Debate:** does Stoic ethics rest on cosmic nature (Striker, Cooper) or can it stand on human nature alone (Annas)?

- Primary: LS 63, The end and happiness (pp. 394-400 †). Existing: DL VII.84-89; Meditations V.1, VIII.7.
- Core: Striker ch. 12, "Following nature: A study in Stoic ethics" (pp. 221-280). Cooper RE ch. 20, "Eudaimonism, the Appeal to Nature, and 'Moral Duty' in Stoicism" (pp. 427-448). Annas MH ch. 5, "The Stoics: Human Nature and the Point of View of the Universe" (pp. 159-179).
- Further: Brennan ch. 9, "Final Ends" (pp. 134-153); Long SS ch. 8, "Stoic eudaimonism" (pp. 179-201 †); CHHP ch. 21 §§ i-iv (pp. 675-686).
- Note: these readings bear on the course's "three senses of nature" (see the audit: individual nature is Panaetius' personae, not DL VII.87-89).

### 11. Seminar: the examined life

Synthesis week; nothing new in the ancient texts.

- Core: Companion ch. 10, Brennan, "Stoic Moral Psychology" (pp. 257-294), if not read in session 7. Long SS ch. 6, "The logical basis of Stoic ethics" (pp. 134-155 †).

### 12. Paper workshop: thesis, evidence, objection

A lighter week, for writing. The reading is a model of how to use a doxographical source, which every Stoic ethics paper must do.

- Primary: existing Seneca, Ep. 33 and 84; Ench. 49.
- Core: Long SS ch. 5, "Arius Didymus and the exposition of Stoic ethics" (pp. 107-133 †).

### 13. Final seminar: synthesis and objections

**Debate:** the sufficiency of virtue against its strongest critics: Antiochus and the Peripatetics in Cicero's *De Finibus* IV, Carneades, and the charge that the Stoics changed the words and kept the substance.

- Primary: LS 64, The end: Academic criticism and Stoic defence (pp. 401-409 †). Existing: *De Finibus* III and IV; NE I.8-10; *De Fato* 28-30, 40-43; Plutarch, *On Common Conceptions*.
- Core: Annas MH ch. 19, "Theophrastus and the Stoics: Forcing the Issue" (pp. 385-411). Norms ch. 8, Irwin, "Stoic and Aristotelian conceptions of happiness" (pp. 205-244 †). Striker ch. 14, "Antipater, or the art of living" (pp. 298-315). Companion ch. 14, Irwin, "Stoic Naturalism and Its Critics" (pp. 345-364).
- Further: existing Nussbaum, *The Therapy of Desire* ch. 10 (pages unverified); Annas MH ch. 20, "Aristotelian Responses" (pp. 412-425).

### 14. Qualifying conversation

No new reading. The viva may draw on any core item above.
